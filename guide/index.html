How OhanaLedger works

A guide to keeping the household’s money and the family’s health records in the app, organised by what you want to do. Everything described here happens on your phone; nothing is sent to us.

Getting started

The first time you open the app it asks how you are starting.

A new household

  1. Tap Start a new household.
  2. Enter your name. A first name is enough; the rest can be added later.
  3. Add the first place money is kept, for example your wallet, with roughly what is in it now.
  4. Tap Open Today. You can skip either step and come back to it; Today lists what was put off.

Joining a household already using the app

If your partner already keeps the household’s records on their phone:

  1. On their phone, open Settings → Key and recovery code and show the recovery code.
  2. On your phone, tap Join a household already using it and enter that code.
  3. On their phone, write a backup (Settings → Backup → Write and share) and send the file to yourself.
  4. On your phone, restore that file. Both phones now show the same key fingerprint.

The two phones do not sync. Each backup is a snapshot; to bring the other phone up to date, write and restore a new one.

Finding your way around

Four tabs run along the bottom of the screen.

  • Today shows what the household holds, what went out this month, anything that needs you today (a bill, a dose, a paper running out), what is coming up, and the latest entries.
  • Money holds the accounts and every money tool: the ledger, reports, transfers, goals, bills, income and the forecast.
  • Health lists each person’s record, doses due, growth charts, pregnancy, appointments and doctors.
  • Settings covers the household, categories, currency, reminders, the lock, backups, and recently deleted items.

Tapping an existing record opens it to read. To change it, tap Edit at the top. The phone’s Back button or gesture always closes a form without saving.

Accounts

Money → Manage accounts → Add

  • Choose a type: Cash, Bank, E-wallet (GCash, Maya), Credit or Investment.
  • Enter the balance right now. For a credit card, choose Owes this so it counts as money owed.
  • Set who it belongs to, or leave it Shared. Money groups the totals by owner.

An account that has entries cannot be deleted, because past months would stop adding up. Close account instead: it leaves the active list and its history stays.

Recording money

Today → Money, or Money → Record

An expense or income

  1. Choose Money out or Money in, and type the amount.
  2. Pick the account, the category, who was paid (Paid to), and the date (today unless you change it).
  3. For money out, say Who was it for?: the Household (already chosen), one person, or Split it. Check Paid by underneath.
  4. Optionally tap More details to add a note and a photo of the receipt.
  5. Tap Save at the top, or Record entry at the bottom.

Type a new name under Paid to to save it. Next time you pick that payee, its usual category and account are filled in for you. Picking someone who usually pays you, such as an employer, switches the entry to Money in.

A receipt for more than one person

Tap Split it. There is a part for each parent: type what was theirs, and the Household takes whatever is left, so a mostly shared receipt needs only one or two figures. Tap a name to change who a part was for, and Add an item to list items one by one with what they were.

Underneath, the app says who owes whom on that receipt, for example Dado owes Tala ₱300.00. The household's share is divided the way Settings → Contribution says, and a child's items count as the household's. Nothing is owed when the payer is not recorded.

Next time you pick the same Paid to, the entry starts the way the last one there went: the household's, one person's, or split.

Closing a form you have filled in asks before throwing it away.

When the account cannot cover it

Before saving, the app warns you if the expense would take a wallet, bank or e-wallet below zero, or dip into money set aside for a savings goal. Choose Change it to fix the amount, or Spend anyway. If savings were affected, the app then asks which goal gives up the difference. Credit cards are never warned about, since they are meant to go negative.

Moving money between your own accounts

Money → Move money

Use a transfer, for example cash withdrawn from the bank, rather than two entries. A transfer is not spending, so it never shows up in your spending reports. The screen shows both balances after the move before you confirm.

Bills and paydays

Recurring expenses

Money → Recurring expenses → Add

Set the name (Meralco, water, wifi), roughly what it costs, how often (monthly, weekly or yearly), the next due date, the usual account and category, and how many days ahead to be reminded. Mark Paid automatically for autopay.

To pay one, tap it and Record payment. Enter what you actually paid. The entry goes on the ledger and the due date moves on, together. A bill due on the 31st moves to the last day of shorter months.

Expected income

Money → Coming in

Add paydays and other regular income the same way. When it arrives, tap it and Record it with the amount that actually came in. On the day it is due, it also appears on Today.

Between now and payday

Money → Between now and payday

A forecast of the next 45 days: what you hold, minus money set aside for goals, plus expected income, minus bills. The chart shows the lowest point and whether anything falls below zero. It is an estimate, not the ledger.

Saving for something

Money → Saving for → Add

  • Name the goal, enter what it costs, choose the account the money is kept in, and optionally a target date. With a date, the goal shows how much to put aside each month.
  • Set aside what is already there marks money already in the account as being for this goal. Nothing moves.
  • Move money in from another account transfers money into the goal’s account.
  • When you buy the thing, tap I bought it.

Several goals can share one account. If spending leaves the account holding less than its goals have set aside, Saving for shows a warning; Sort out lets you decide which goal gives way.

Seeing where money went

  • Transactions: every entry by day, for a month or a year, with money in, money out and what is left. Use the arrows to step through periods.
  • Where it went: a breakdown by category for money out, money in, or both. A heading such as Transportation includes everything filed under it.
  • Spending over time: spending by day, week, month or year, or a range you pick.

Categories go two levels deep, for example Transport and under it Grab. Manage them in Settings → Categories.

Each category can have an icon, which is shown beside its entries everywhere in the ledger. Open a category and pick one from the grid. Tap the chosen icon again to clear it. A sub-category with no icon of its own uses its heading's.

People in the household

Settings → Household members → Add

  • Names are entered in parts: first, middle, last and suffix. A short name, if set, is what shows on rows.
  • Mark each person as a parent or other, and adult or minor. Add a date of birth and sex for children; the growth charts need both.
  • For an adult, add a phone number and turn on Call in an emergency to list them on the emergency card. Children are never offered as someone to call.

If an emergency contact has no number saved, Settings points it out so the card is not left without anyone to ring.

Health records

Health → tap a person

Each person has one record with sections for visits, medications, allergies, conditions, immunisations and measurements. Use the Add button beside a section to add to it.

Allergies

You must choose a severity before an allergy can be saved, because it decides who sees it. Severe allergies are pinned to the top of the person’s page and shown first on the emergency card. Moderate ones are also on the card; mild ones stay on the record only.

Visits and appointments

Add a visit with its date, the doctor, the reason, and afterwards what was found and the plan. A future date makes it an appointment, with a reminder. Turn on Record a cost to put what it cost on the ledger under Health. All visits are listed in Health → Appointments; open one to move or cancel it.

Measurements and growth

Record weight, height, temperature, blood pressure and more. For children, Health → Growth plots weight, height and BMI against the WHO reference and gives a percentile. A percentile is a position, not a verdict; one reading is not a trend.

Pregnancy

Health → Pregnancy → Add

Enter the due date, or the first day of the last period. How far along is worked out from it every day, and weights already recorded are plotted.

Doctors and clinics

Health → Doctors and clinics

Save each doctor with a phone number. The call button opens the phone’s dialler with the number ready.

Medication and doses

  1. Add a medication to a person with its dose and how often.
  2. Under Doses a day, choose once, twice, three or four times a day, or set your own times.
  3. Each dose then appears in Health → Doses and on Today when it is due. Tap it and answer Taken, or Let this one go to record it as deliberately skipped.

If a medicine’s name contains something the person is recorded as allergic to, the app asks before saving. It only compares names, so it cannot tell that amoxicillin is a penicillin. Always check with a doctor or pharmacist.

The emergency card

Health → Emergency

One page for someone helping in an emergency: each person’s age and blood type, severe and moderate allergies, current medication, ongoing conditions, and who to call with a tap. It can be opened from the lock screen without unlocking the app, and it offers no way into anything else.

Papers and expiry dates

Settings → Papers → Add

Keep passports, licences, registrations and similar papers with their number, whose they are, the issue and expiry dates, and a photo. The app reminds you before one runs out. When you renew, edit the same paper with the new dates rather than adding another.

Backups and a new phone

Settings → Backup

Writing a backup

  1. Leave Include photos on to carry receipts and scans, or turn it off for a smaller file.
  2. Tap Write and share, then save the file somewhere safe, such as Google Drive.

The file is encrypted. Only a phone holding your recovery code can open it. Settings shows how many changes are not in a backup yet.

Moving to a new phone

  1. On the old phone, write a backup and note the recovery code from Settings → Key and recovery code.
  2. On the new phone, install OhanaLedger, choose Join a household already using it, and enter the code.
  3. Restore the backup file. Photos already on the phone are kept; missing ones are added.

Keep the recovery code somewhere safe and private. Without it, a backup cannot be opened. With it, anyone holding your backup file can read it.

Lock, privacy and deleting

  • Fingerprint lock (Settings) asks for your fingerprint when the app opens and after it has been away for a minute.
  • Celebrations (Settings) are the small moments when you finish something: a stamp when a bill is paid, a tick when a dose is taken, a line when a savings goal passes a quarter, a half or three quarters, and a look back at last month during the first week of each month. Turn the switch off for none of them.
  • Theme (Settings) changes how the app looks. "Same as the phone" switches between Light and Dark with the phone. The others stay as you set them: Light, Dark, Sampaguita, Forest, Ocean, Summer, Winter, Mochi and Blossom.
  • Hide balances covers what the household holds, useful when someone is looking over your shoulder. Tap the hidden figure on Today to show it.
  • Anything you delete goes to Settings → Recently deleted for 30 days (you can change this). Open it to Put it back or Delete for good.
  • Settings → Export makes a spreadsheet of the ledger or a PDF of a person’s health record. Exports are not encrypted.
  • Ads appear only at the bottom of some overview pages, never on health pages. In Europe and the UK you can change your ad choices in Settings → Ad privacy choices.

Common questions

Where is my data kept?
Only on your phone. There is no account and no server. The only copies elsewhere are backup files you save yourself, and your phone’s own Android backup if that is turned on.
What happens if I lose my phone?
Install the app on a new phone, choose Join a household, enter your recovery code, and restore your latest backup file. Anything recorded after that backup is lost, so back up regularly.
Do two phones stay in sync?
No. A backup is a snapshot. The phone you restore it on matches the file at that moment.
Why doesn’t a transfer count as spending?
Moving money from one of your accounts to another does not change what the household has, so it is left out of spending reports.
Why can’t I delete an account or category?
Because entries are filed under it, and deleting it would change past months. Close the account, or hide the category, instead.
I deleted something by mistake.
Right after deleting, tap Undo on the message at the bottom of the screen. Later, open Settings → Recently deleted and tap Put it back. Items stay there for 30 days unless you change the setting.
Can I use a currency other than pesos?
Yes, in Settings → Currency. It changes how figures are written, not the amounts themselves.
